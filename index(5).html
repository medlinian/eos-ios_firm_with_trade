Markup Pricing, Trade, and Economies of Scale

This is a markup-pricing model of a firm under trade. The firm sets a markup price over average cost at a normal/planned output level, while actual sales are determined by firm demand, subject to productive capacity. The graph illustrates internal economies of scale through a downward-sloping average-cost curve over the relevant output range. As the industry expands, external economies can shift the AC curve downward. Holding the markup constant, a lower AC implies a lower markup price. AC = P only if free entry/import competition completely competes away economic profit.

Markup parameter θ. A larger markup raises the markup price and lowers quantity demanded at that price.
Trade exposure. Moving right represents stronger export-market access (firm demand shifts right). Moving left represents stronger import competition.
Industry expansion / external economies. A larger industry shifts the firm's AC curve downward; holding θ constant, that lowers the markup price.
Interpretation. The horizontal line is the firm's markup price—an markup price set as a markup over normal/planned average cost. Capacity is located at the minimum of the average-cost curve—the point just before AC begins to rise. The firm is demand-constrained whenever sales are below that capacity level. Import competition reduces the firm's firm demand; export-market access raises it. There is no tangency or MR = MC condition.
